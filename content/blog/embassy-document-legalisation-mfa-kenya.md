## Why Do Kenyan Documents Require Legalisation?

When presenting Kenyan official records abroad—for international employment (e.g. UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar), university admissions (e.g. Germany, Italy, China), immigration, or cross-border marriages—foreign authorities require verification that the seal, stamp, and signature on your Kenyan document are genuine and lawfully issued.

Because Kenya is not currently a signatory to the Hague Apostille Convention of 1961, Kenyan public records must undergo a multi-tier **chain legalisation process** ending in bilateral embassy attestation.

## The Standard 4-Stage Legalisation Chain in Kenya

### Stage 1: Issuing Authority Verification
Before any statutory document can be authenticated by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MFA), it must originate from or be certified by the appropriate primary regulatory body:
- **Academic Degrees & Transcripts:** Certified by the Commission for University Education (CUE) or Kenya National Examinations Council (KNEC).
- **Civil Certificates (Birth, Marriage, Death):** Certified copy issued by the Civil Registration Services (CRS) or Registrar of Marriages (Sheria House).
- **Police Clearance Certificate (Good Conduct):** Verified via the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) / eCitizen QR validation.
- **Commercial Documents (CR12, Articles of Association, Resolutions):** Certified by the Business Registration Service (BRS) or Kenya National Chamber of Commerce and Industry (KNCCI).

### Stage 2: Notary Public & High Court Certification
Private legal documents (such as Powers of Attorney, Affidavits, Sponsorship Letters, or Commercial Contracts) must be signed and sealed by an appointed **Notary Public (Advocate of the High Court of Kenya)**, and subsequently verified by the Registrar of the High Court of Kenya.

### Stage 3: Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MFA) Authentication
Documents are presented to the Legal and Consular Directorate of the **Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Trade (Old Treasury Building, Harambee Avenue, Nairobi)**.
- MFA officials inspect the underlying signatures against their official specimen registers.
- An official MFA seal, embossed stamp, and authorized signature are appended to the reverse side of the document.

### Stage 4: Embassy Consular Legalisation / Attestation
Following MFA authentication, the document is submitted to the **Consular Section of the recipient foreign embassy in Nairobi** (e.g., UAE Embassy, Saudi Embassy, Chinese Embassy, Italian Embassy, or Qatar Embassy).
- The embassy verifies the MFA seal and applies their official consular certification stamp.

## Certified Sworn Translations

If your destination country has an official language other than English (e.g. France, Germany, Italy, Spain, China, Saudi Arabia), you must obtain a **Certified Sworn Translation** by an accredited translator (recognized by the respective embassy or Alliance Française / Goethe-Institut) before final consular legalisation.
